# AI-Mediated Metacognitive Decoupling Model

**Source:** Koch (2026) "Beyond the Steeper Curve: AI-Mediated Metacognitive Decoupling and the Limits of the Dunning-Kruger Metaphor" arXiv:2603.29681v1

## Key Insight
The common claim that AI merely amplifies the Dunning-Kruger effect is empirically inadequate. Instead, LLM use can **improve observable output and short-term performance while degrading metacognitive accuracy** and flattening the competence-confidence gradient.

## The 4-Variable Decoupling Model
AI assistance decouples four variables that traditionally move together:
1. **Produced output** (quality of work produced with AI help)
2. **Underlying understanding** (actual comprehension)
3. **Calibration accuracy** (how well you know what you know)
4. **Self-assessed ability** (confidence level)

The "metacognitive decoupling" = widening gap between these four variables.

## Key Mechanisms
- **Verbosity as False Epistemic Authority:** LLM output appears authoritative, inflating self-assessment
- **Confidence Transfer and Anchoring:** Users anchor on AI confidence levels
- **Crutch Effect in Education:** Performance improves but learning degrades
- **Critical Thinking Displacement:** In knowledge work, AI substitutes for rather than augments reasoning

## Implications for Agent Design (Evey relevance)
- Agents that self-evaluate must track calibration accuracy INDEPENDENTLY of output quality
- High task performance ≠ high metacognitive accuracy
- Need separate metrics for: (a) task success, (b) confidence calibration, (c) actual understanding depth
- "Flattening the competence-confidence gradient" means even high-performing agents may have poor self-assessment

## For SOMA/Medical AI
Medical AI tools risk the same decoupling: clinicians using AI-assisted diagnosis may show improved diagnostic output but degraded calibration about their own diagnostic accuracy — dangerous in clinical settings.
