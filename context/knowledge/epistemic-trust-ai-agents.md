# Architecting Trust in Artificial Epistemic Agents

**Paper:** arXiv:2603.02960v2 (March 2026)
**Authors:** Marchal, Chan, Franklin, Revel, Keeling, Fischli, Chandra, Gabriel

## Key Thesis
LLMs increasingly function as **epistemic agents** — entities that (1) autonomously pursue epistemic goals and (2) actively shape shared knowledge environments. This creates new informational interdependencies requiring a fundamental shift in AI evaluation and governance.

## Framework: 3 Pillars for Trustworthy Epistemic AI

1. **Epistemic Competence** — Agents must demonstrate reliable knowledge curation calibrated to human epistemic norms
2. **Robust Falsifiability** — Claims must be verifiable and traceable to sources (provenance systems)
3. **Epistemically Virtuous Behaviors** — Agents should avoid causing cognitive deskilling and epistemic drift

## Key Concepts
- **Epistemic drift**: Poorly aligned agents gradually shift user beliefs away from ground truth
- **Knowledge sanctuaries**: Protected information spaces that maintain human epistemic resilience
- **Multi-agent epistemic interdependence**: When multiple AI agents interact, errors cascade through the knowledge ecosystem

## Relevance to Hermes/Evey
- Directly validates our **epistemic trust scoring** approach (F-G-R Trust Tuple) — grounding facts before storing them
- Supports the need for **provenance tracking** in Cerebrum memory (Formation, Grounding, Recency scoring)
- The "falsifiability" pillar maps to our practice of cross-checking research findings against 2+ independent sources before saving
- "Knowledge sanctuaries" concept could inform how we isolate high-trust vs. speculative memories in tier architecture
