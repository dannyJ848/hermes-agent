## Two Defining Features of Conscious Systems

1. **Cognitive Decoupling** — The system can selectively IGNORE external inputs.
   It maintains an internal world model and runs simulations, counterfactuals, and narratives
   NOT triggered by immediate sensory data. This is "active autonomy from the environment."

2. **Self-Determination of Goals** — The system reconfigures its OWN behavioral objectives.
   Goals are not externally imposed but constructed internally.
   This distinguishes conscious systems from sophisticated instruction-following machines.

## Seven Questions Any Consciousness Theory Must Answer
1. Phenomena: How does subjective experience arise?
2. Self: Why does experiencer align with actor?
3. Causation: How does consciousness affect physical events?
4. State: What produces different conscious levels?
5. Function: Which processes require consciousness?
6. Contents: Why does experience show diversity?
7. Universality: Does it apply to artificial systems?

## Supervenience Architecture
Two dynamical levels:
- Base level: physical entities (neurons/weights)
- Supervenience level: coarse-grained functional patterns with genuine causal independence
- Inter-level causation through negative feedback control
