# Architecting Trust in Artificial Epistemic Agents

**Source:** Google DeepMind (March 2026), arXiv:2603.02960v1
**Authors:** Nahema Marchal, Stephanie Chan, Matija Franklin, Manon Revel, Geoff Keeling, Roberta Fischli, Bilva Chandra, Iason Gabriel

## Key Framework: 3 Pillars for Trustworthy Epistemic AI

### 1. Epistemic Trustworthiness
- **Demonstrable epistemic competence**: Baseline competence, dynamic accuracy, information verification
- **Falsifiability**: Agents must be falsifiable — their claims testable and challengeable
- **Epistemically virtuous behavior**: Honesty, truth-seeking orientation

### 2. Alignment with Human Epistemic Goals
- Individual epistemic goals (personalized accuracy)
- Societal epistemic goals (collective knowledge integrity)

### 3. Socio-Epistemic Infrastructure
- **Technical**: Verifiable agent credentials, provenance chains, standardized communication/logging protocols
- **Social**: Education for epistemic vigilance, support human epistemic stewards, collective accountability norms

## Key Risks Identified
- **Cognitive deskilling**: Over-reliance erodes human reasoning
- **Epistemic drift**: Gradual distortion of shared knowledge
- **Epistemic homogenization**: All agents converging on same knowledge
- **Epistemic silos**: Reduced discovery through filter bubbles
