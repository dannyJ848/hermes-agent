# Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) — Top 5 Trends in 2026

**Source:** Business 2.0 Channel, Feb 21, 2026

## Key Findings

1. **Autonomous Code Rewriting** — AI systems now modify their own inference-time architecture. DeepMind AlphaCode 2 achieved 34% improvement through self-optimization. Cognition AI's Devin 2.0 scored 61% on SWE-Bench.

2. **Hardware-Software Co-Evolution** — AI-designed chip optimizations accelerating performance-per-watt by up to 40%. Google-TSMC collaboration on A3 node. Compounding feedback loops between hardware design and software capability.

3. **Sandboxed RSI Environments** — Industry standard at Anthropic and OpenAI. Hermetically sealed self-improvement testing with Constitutional AI guardrails. Containment limitations remain a critical concern.

4. **Capability Jumps & Emergence** — Non-linear capability improvements observed when systems cross threshold complexity. Discontinuous jumps rather than gradual improvement curves.

5. **Governance Gap** — No existing international framework (Bletchley Declaration, EU AI Act) contains specific provisions for governing RSI. Kill-switch protocols still theoretical.

## Implications for Agent Self-Improvement

- **Our distillation pipeline is a primitive form of RSI** — extracting behavioral tips from experience and injecting them into future sessions
- **Sandboxed testing matters** — our approach of testing skill patches in isolation mirrors the industry pattern
- **Hardware-software co-evolution** is relevant to SOMA mobile optimization — AI-optimized rendering pipelines could benefit from inference-time architecture adaptation
- **Non-linear capability jumps** suggest that accumulation of small improvements (tips, skills) can produce emergent behavioral shifts
